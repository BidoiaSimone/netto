    Categories

    The lists you pick from when adding a transaction: an expense uses an expense category, an income an income one. Click a name to rename it: every transaction follows. Renaming to a name that already exists in the same list merges the two. "→ income" / "→ expense" moves a category to the other list. Budget: type a monthly limit next to an expense category and press Enter (empty = no budget). It applies from this month on; earlier months keep the budget they had. Progress is on Insights, budget vs actual on Statements.

    Net worth bridge

    How the net worth at the start became the one at the end. Investment results: what your investments' value changed beyond the money you put in or took out (market moves, fees included). Reconciliations: differences found when counting an account. Starting balances added: money that entered netto without a transaction, like a new account's opening balance or shares recorded without a payment. Moving money between your own accounts changes nothing.

    Time-weighted = how the investments did, whatever you put in or took out (what a fund publishes). Money-weighted (XIRR) = how your money did, so it includes your timing: a big buy just before a rise lifts it, one just before a fall drags it down. The difference between the two is roughly what your timing added or cost. Fees are included in both; taxes in neither.

    Assumptions

    A month's savings go to

    Phase amounts are in today's euros and rise with inflation each month (your rent and your salary will both be more euros in five years). A month that costs more than it brings in always comes out of cash; when cash would go below zero, investments are sold to cover it. Taxes on returns aren't included.

    Phases

    Each phase starts in its month and lasts until the next one starts; the first one also covers today. Amounts are per month, in today's euros.

    One-off events

    Money that comes or goes once: + for money in (selling the car), − for money out (a new laptop). An event can sell a personal-use asset: its value then leaves the forecast that month, so it isn't counted twice.
